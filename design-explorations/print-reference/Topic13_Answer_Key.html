Lesson 13-4 — Identify Lines of Symmetry
1.
Triangle with vertical dashed line — does it show a line of symmetry?
Yes No
A vertical line through the apex of an equilateral triangle divides it into two identical mirror halves.
2.
Trapezoid with horizontal dashed line — does it show a line of symmetry?
Yes No
The top is narrower than the bottom — folding along the horizontal line does not produce matching halves.
3. Apply Math — Is the butterfly symmetrical?
Answer
Yes — it is symmetrical
A vertical line through the body divides the butterfly into two identical mirror-image halves. Both wings (upper and lower) match on each side.
4. Is the horizontal line through the irregular figure a line of symmetry?
Answer
No — not a line of symmetry
The shape above the line is a different size and shape than the portion below it. Folding along the line would not produce two identical halves.
5. Higher Order Thinking — Are ALL letters in MARK symmetrical?
Answer
No — Mark is NOT fully correct
M ✓ has vertical symmetry  |  A ✓ has vertical symmetry  |  R ✗ no line of symmetry  |  K ✗ no line of symmetry. Only M and A are symmetrical.
6. Assessment Practice — Which figure shows a line of symmetry?
A
Rectangle + diagonal
C
Hexagon + vertical line ← CORRECT
B
Irregular quad + vertical
D
Parallelogram + horizontal
A regular hexagon's vertical line of symmetry divides it into two perfect mirror halves. The other shapes' lines do not create matching halves.

Lesson 13-5 — Find Perimeter
3. Apply Math — Tablet 9 units long × 5 units wide. Find the perimeter.
Work
9 + 9 + 5 + 5 = 28
or: 2 × (9 + 5) = 2 × 14 = 28
Answer
Perimeter = 28 units
4. Higher Order Thinking — Frame is 2 units longer on each side. Original: 3 wide × 5 tall. Find perimeter of new frame.
Work
New width: 3 + 2 = 5 units
New height: 5 + 2 = 7 units
Perimeter: 5 + 5 + 7 + 7 = 24 units
Answer
Perimeter = 24 units
Each side gets 2 units longer, so width goes from 3→5 and height goes from 5→7. Then add all 4 sides.

Lesson 13-6 — Find Perimeter
1. Rectangle 8 cm × 5 cm
Work
8 + 8 + 5 + 5 = 26
Answer
26 cm
2. Regular hexagon, all sides = 6 m
Work
6 × 6 = 36
Answer
36 m
3. Apply Math — Window 9 ft tall × 4 ft wide, NO border on bottom.
Work
Left side + Right side + Top only
9 + 9 + 4 = 22 ft
Answer
22 feet
Since there is no border on the bottom, only 3 sides are counted: both vertical sides (9 ft each) and the top (4 ft).
4. Explain — Carlos vs. Maria. Sticker: 9+9+2+2+4 = 26 cm. Who is correct?
Answer
Maria is correct. Perimeter = 26 cm
Explanation
Maria: 9 + 9 + 2 + 2 + 4 = 26 cm ✓ (includes all 5 sides)
Carlos: 9 + 2 + 2 + 4 = 17 ✗ (forgot the bottom 9 cm side)
5. Higher Order Thinking — Hexagon P=48 and Square P=48. How many more inches per side for the square?
Work
Hexagon: 48 ÷ 6 = 8 inches per side
Square: 48 ÷ 4 = 12 inches per side
Difference: 12 − 8 = 4 inches
Answer
4 inches greater

Lesson 13-7 — Garden Beds Performance Task
Reference: The garden bed design shown is 9 ft × 7 ft. Perimeter = 9 + 9 + 7 + 7 = 32 ft. All other valid designs must also total 32 ft.
1. Draw other garden bed designs with the same amount of fencing (P = 32 ft).
Any rectangle whose sides add up to 32 ft is acceptable. Sample valid designs:
8+8+8+8 = 32 ✓
10+10+6+6 = 32 ✓
12+12+4+4 = 32 ✓
2. Be Efficient — How much fencing is used? How do you know?
Work
9 + 9 + 7 + 7 = 32 ft
I know because I added all four sides of the rectangle together.
Answer
32 feet of fencing
3. Explain — Jake says a square with 8 ft sides uses the same fencing. Is he correct?
Work
Square with 8 ft sides: 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 = 32 ft ✓
32 ft = 32 ft → same amount of fencing
Answer
Yes — Jake is correct
A square with 8 ft sides has a perimeter of 32 ft, which equals the perimeter of the 9×7 garden bed design.
4. Make Sense — Describe one other garden bed that could be made.
Sample Answer
A 10 ft × 6 ft garden bed
Explanation
10 + 10 + 6 + 6 = 32 ft ✓
This rectangle uses the same 32 ft of fencing as the original design.
Other valid answers: 11×5, 12×4, 13×3, 14×2, 15×1, or 8×8 (square).
